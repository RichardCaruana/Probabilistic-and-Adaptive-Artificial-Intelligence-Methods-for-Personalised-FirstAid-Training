1.2 HOW TO MINIMISE THE RISK
OF INFECTION TO SELF AND OTHERS

It is important to protect yourself and
the casualty from the risk of infection
You can do this by:
•

Maintaining good hygiene

•

Wash your hands

•

Always wear suitable gloves

•

Place a barrier between yourself
and any of the casualty's bodily fluids

•

Use a face shield when giving resuscitation

•

Only use clean, sterile dressings

•

Dispose of contaminated items safely

1.3 THE NEED FOR ESTABLISHING CONSENT
TO PROVIDE FIRST AID
•

You should ask for and receive a casualty’s consent to
your treatment

•

Parental consent should be sought
in the case of a minor

•

If the casualty is unconscious,
or is unable to give consent
due to their injuries, you can
assume consent
and commence treatment

•

You should not commence
treatment if a casualty refuses
your offer

© AOFAQ 2017

19

1.4 ACTIONS TO TAKE FOLLOWING
AN ACCIDENT/INCIDENT
•

The law requires employers to record and report
certain types of accidents and injuries in the
workplace

•

It is your responsibility
to report any accident
to your immediate supervisor
or manager.

•

You must complete
appropriate documentation
e.g. the accident book.

© AOFAQ 2017

20

1.4 THE ACCIDENT REPORT FORM

•

All accidents at work must be recorded in an
accident book

•

This information is used
by the employer to identify risks,
improvements in health and safety
and first aid provision

•

It may also be used
in insurance investigations

•

It is also important to record
if the casualty refuses treatment

© AOFAQ 2017
