3.3 THE IMPORTANCE OF PLACING
A CASUALTY IN THE RECOVERY POSITION

It is important to place the casualty in the
Recovery Position because it:
•

Aids the maintenance of
an open airway

•

Draws away fluids from
the mouth such as vomit
or saliva

© AOFAQ 2017

56

3.4 PLACE AN UNRESPONSIVE CASUALTY
IN THE RECOVERY POSITION (PRACTICAL)
•

Key points

•

Never lay an unconscious person on their back
as this heightens the risk of obstructing the airway

•

Pregnant women should be placed on their left hand side
unless the injury makes this impossible

•

If the casualty is going to be in the position for more than 30
minutes, you should move them to the other side. This is to
prevent fluid build up. Only do this if the casualty does not have
other injuries.

•

If the casualty has glasses, remove
them and keep them safe

•

Check the casualties pockets before
placing them the Recovery Position
and keep the contents safe.

3.5 MANAGE A CASUALTY WHO IS IN SEIZURE

Seizures are caused by sudden, abnormal
electrical activity in the brain.
• Seizures can be caused by
reactions to medicines,
high fevers, head injuries
and certain diseases such as cancer
of the brain or epilepsy can result in a seizure.
•

There are two types of seizure:
• Tonic-clonic (referred
to a ‘fit’ or ‘Grand-Mal’)
• Absences (sometimes
referred to as ‘Petit-Mal’)
© AOFAQ 2017

59

3.5 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS
OF A SEIZURE (EXAMPLES)
•

Sudden loss of consciousness

•

Staring blankly ahead

•

Odd noises or movements/facial expressions

•

Muscle rigidity followed
by uncontrolled jerking

•

Breathing ceases but resumes
once seizure has finished

•

Clenched jaw and bloody
saliva- casualty may bite tongue

•

Incontinence

© AOFAQ 2017

3.5 MANAGING A CASUALTY WHO IS IN SEIZURE

•

Help the casualty to the floor to prevent injury (if
they have not already fallen). Note the time of the
seizure

•

Remove any potential dangers

•

Remove any bystanders

•

Protect the head

•

Loosen any tight clothing

•

Wait, monitor and constantly reassure the casualty

Call 999/112 if this is the casualty's first ever
seizure, if the episode lasts for more than 5 minutes
or2017
if the casualty has been unconscious for more
