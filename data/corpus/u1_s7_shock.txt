7.1 RECOGNISE SHOCK

•

Shock is a life threatening condition and should be
given top priority treatment, second only to airway
management and CPR.

•

It is caused when the circulatory
system is disturbed and fails.

•

This results in the organs of the
body being subjected
to oxygen starvation.

•

The most common cause of shock
is blood loss. Any blood loss greater
than 2 litres will result in shock.

•

Always assume and treat for shock

7.1 EXAMPLES OF CAUSES OF SHOCK

•

Blood loss

•

Heart attack

•

Severe burns

•

Witnessing an accident

•

Severe vomiting

•

Poisoning

•

Head trauma

7.2 ADMINISTER FIRST
TO A CASUALTY WHO IS IN SHOCK

The signs and symptoms of shock are:
•

Rapid or weak pulse accompanied by shallow or
rapid breathing

•

Pale, cold and clammy skin

•

Cyanosis- blue skin

•

Weakness and dizziness

•

Vomiting and or nausea

•

The casualty may exhibit
signs of confusion
or aggression/agitation

© AOFAQ 2017

114

7.2 TREATMENT OF SHOCK

•

If the casualty is conscious lie them down, elevating the
legs, bending at the knees.
Keeping the head low.

•

If the casualty is unconsciousplace in the recovery position.

•

Loosen any tight clothing.

•

Protect from the elements.

•

Raise the legs.

•

Monitor and record levels
of consciousness and breathing.

•

Call 999/112 if the injury and/or illness is severe or the
casualty deteriorates.
