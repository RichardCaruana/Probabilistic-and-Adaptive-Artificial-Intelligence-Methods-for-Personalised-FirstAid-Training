8.1 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH SMALL CUTS, GRAZES AND BRUISES
•

Minor cuts and bruises are treated quite easily

•

The body deals with minor injuries really well causing
clotting to take place in the ruptured
or torn blood vessels

•

Apply the basic principles of hygiene
when cleaning and dressing
the wound will aid this process

•

Bruising is bleeding from blood
vessels under the skin.

•

If bruising is severe or accompanies
swelling it may be indicative
of a deeper wound or fracture

© AOFAQ 2017

8.1 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH SMALL CUTS, GRAZES AND BRUISES

Cuts and grazes
•

Wash your hands and put on gloves

•

Wash the wound with cold water
(or use alcohol free wipes)

•

Wipe from the centre of the
wound outwards, use one
swap for each wipe, pat dry

•

Wash the area around the wound
with warm water and soap, avoiding
the wound itself.
Cover with a plaster or dressing as appropriate

8.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH MINOR BURNS AND SCALDS
•

Minor burns and scalds are usually caused by domestic
incidents such as spilling
boiling liquid onto the skin, touching
a hot surface like a hot cooker etc

•

They result in a burn or scald

•

Such injuries usually result
in reddening of the skin and perhaps
later, a small blister

•

These types of injuries can usually be treated successfully
by first aid and will heal naturally

•

If you are concerned about the seriousness of the burn or
scald, you should advise the casualty to seek medical
advice.

© AOFAQ 2017

8.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH MINOR BURNS AND SCALDS

•Cool
•Cool the injury site with cold running water for at least 10 minutes
•Do not use Ice
•Do not apply any fats, creams or lotions
•Remove
•Remove any restricting items such as jewellery before the area starts to swell
•Dress
•When the area is cooled sufficiently cover the burn to minimise risk of infection
•Cling film is ideal or sterile non-fluffy dressings

8.3 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH SMALL SPLINTERS
•

Splinters are foreign objects which embed
themselves into the skin

•

They can do this either fully or partially

•

Common causes of splinters include
glass, wood, plastic and metal

Recognition features include:
•

Possible pain at the site of injury

•

A splinter is visible

•

Possible swelling at the injury site

•

Possibility of bleeding

© AOFAQ 2017

8.3 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH SMALL SPLINTERS

Treatment (partially embedded)
•

Put on disposable gloves

•

Clean the area around the splinter site

•

Draw the splinter out
in direction the splinter entered

•

Ensure the tweezers
are sterile

•

Clean the area with either soap
and water or using a non alcohol wipe

•

Monitor for any signs of infection

8.3 ADMINISTER FIRST AID TO A CASUALTY
WITH SMALL SPLINTERS

Treatment (fully embedded)
•

Put on disposable gloves

•

Clean the area around
the splinter site

•

Place a plaster over
the injury site and leave
overnight

•

Remove the plaster and observe
if the splinter has been drawn out
