## Slide 16

INTRODUCTION TO THE SKELETAL SYSTEM

There are 206 bones in the human body
Bones are also integral to the body’s strength combining with joints and muscles
They act as factories for 
the production of blood 
and essential blood cells 
through bone marrow
Some bones have a protective 
function (skull), some 
a supporting function (pelvis), 
while others are used 
for movement (fingers, jaw)

## Slide 17

2.1  FRACTURE 

A fracture is a crack, chip or break in the bone
A fracture can result from several types of injury e.g. Direct force, indirect force and violent 
or twisting movement
A fracture can occur due to 
pathological reasons e.g. 
The elderly 
Fractures can affect associated 
muscles, tendons, nerves 
and blood vessels where 
these are close to the site of injury

## Slide 18

2.1 TYPES OF  FRACTURE 

Open fracture

Closed fracture

## Slide 19

2.1 RECOGNITION OF FRACTURES 

Pain at the injury site
Possible associated open wound
Swelling and/or bruising 
Pale, cold and clammy skin
Loss of power/movement 
of the limb
Deformity 
Nausea 
Grinding bones

## Slide 20

2.2 TREATMENT OF A FRACTURE 

In the case of an open fracture, control any bleeding by applying pressure around the injury. Take care not to press directly on the protruding bone, place a non-fluffy dressing over the injury. 
Assist the casualty in supporting the fracture in a way that is most comfortable for them.
Arrange to send or take to hospital. Call 999/112 if you are unable to transport. 
If the casualty will allow, immobilise the injured area.

## Slide 22

2.1 DISLOCATIONS 

A dislocation is an injury as a result of a joint socket being separated
Common sites include 
the pelvis and collar bone 
Dislocations are commonly 
caused by sporting accidents, 
muscle contractions, strong 
force or an unnatural 
movement 

## Slide 23

2.1 DISLOCATION RECOGNITION 

Pain at the injury site 
Swelling and/or bruising 
Loss of power/movement 
Deformity 
Signs of shock 

## Slide 24

2.2 TREATMENT OF DISLOCATIONS 

Treat as a fracture 
DO NOT try and put 
the bone back in place 
Arrange for transport 
to the hospital 
Treat for shock 

## Slide 25

2.1 SPRAIN AND STRAIN 

Sprain 

A sprain is often caused by over stretching or a sudden twisting movement which causes an injury to the ligaments which surround a joint. 

Strain

A strain is often is often caused by the over stretching or  an over burden of weight on the muscles and tendons. This causes a tearing effect to the muscles and tendons.

## Slide 26

2.1 SPRAIN AND STRAIN RECOGNITION 

It is very difficult to determine the difference between a sprain and a strain
The recognition features are: 
Pain at the site of injury
Swelling and/or bruising 
Cramp
Loss of power/movement 
in the injured area

## Slide 27

2.2 TREATMENT FOR A SPRAIN/STRAIN 

Rest
Ice
Compression
Elevation

Ice packs should be applied for 10 minutes every 2 hours. 
NEVER allow ice 
to come into direct contact with the skin.
