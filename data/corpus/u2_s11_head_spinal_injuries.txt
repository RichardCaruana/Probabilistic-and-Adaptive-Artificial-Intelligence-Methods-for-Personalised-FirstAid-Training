## Slide 34

3.1 INTRODUCTION TO HEAD INJURIES 

Head injury or suspected head injury should always be taken seriously
Always check the casualty's response for any change in consciousness
The mechanism of injury (MOI) 
is important, as it will help you 
determine the most 
appropriate treatment. 
Remember that a casualty 
who has a head injury may 
also have a spinal/neck injury. 
           Handle with care!

## Slide 35

3.1 THE TYPES OF HEAD INJURIES 

There are three types of head injuries 
Concussion
Compression
Fracture
Some injuries will be 
‘open’ with a visible wound 
and some will be ‘closed’ 
with no outward sign of injury.

## Slide 36

3.1	RECOGNITION 
OF HEAD INJURIES 

Look for:
Mechanism of injury
Skull deformation
Altered or deteriorating response levels sometimes combined with irritability and confusion
Changes in pupils, uneven, pinpoint or dilated
Headache
‘Racoon eyes’ or blackness under and around the eyes
Vomiting or feeling sick
Evidence of a clear discharge from ears or nose (cerebral spinal fluid)
Changes in visual activity (double vision/blurred vision)
‘Snoring’ breathing if unconscious

## Slide 37

3.2 TREATMENT OF HEAD INJURIES 

Maintain the casualty’s airway and breathing 
Clean and treat any visible wounds 
Ensure complete rest
If the casualty is unconscious 
or drowsy, place them 
in the recovery position if you 
don’t suspect spinal injury
Call 999/112 if the injury is severe
Never allow a suspected head injury 
casualty to be left alone

## Slide 38

3.1 SPINAL INJURIES 

It is important if a spinal injury 
is suspected that correct treatment is immediately applied, due to the threat of paralysis or even death of the casualty
Spinal injuries are rare but carefully assessing the mechanism of injury and obtaining a good history will enable the first aider to apply the correct treatment prior to the arrival of the emergency services. 
Always assume spinal injury in the case of high speed road traffic accidents, fall from a height or contact sports injury

## Slide 39

3.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF A HEAD INJURY

The casualty is found in an unnatural posture
Pale and clammy skin
Loss of control of the limbs
Loss of feeling in limbs, 
especially if other obvious 
injuries are present
Pins and needles type 
tingling sensation
Loss of bowel/bladder control
Onset of shock

## Slide 40

3.2 TREATMENT OF SPINAL INJURY'S

Call 999/112
Maintain/open the airway (do not extend the neck)
Take great care to ensure the casualty’s head is immobilised as far as possible and is kept in line with their upper body
Reassure the casualty 
and tell them not to move
DO NOT remove a helmet 
unless CPR is required
