## Slide 47

4.1 RECOGNISING THE DIFFERENT 
TYPES OF CHEST INJURY

Chest injury's are one of the more difficult injuries to adequately manage at a first aid level
Assistance of the emergency services should be sought as soon as possible
First aiders can take steps 
to minimise the discomfort 
caused and prevent 
the casualty being effected 
by shock
Some chest injuries are 
life threatening.

## Slide 48

4.1	RECOGNISING THE DIFFERENT TYPES 
OF CHEST INJURY

The types of chest injuries are: 
Fracture 
Penetrating chest wound 

## Slide 49

4.1	RECOGNISING THE DIFFERENT TYPES 
OF CHEST INJURY (FRACTURE)

Normally caused by direct force to the chest, collapsed objects on the chest etc
The signs and symptoms include: 
Consider the mechanism of injury
Pale and cool skin
Pain, especially when breathing in
Breathing may be rapid and/or shallow
Rapid pulse 

## Slide 50

4.1	RECOGNISING THE DIFFERENT TYPES 
OF CHEST INJURY (PENETRATING CHEST WOUND)

Consider the mechanism of injury
Obvious object e.g. Knife, glass etc
Open wound e.g. Bullet- look for small ‘entry’ wound and large crater ‘exit’ wound
Pale, cool and clammy skin
Raid and weak pulse often accompanied by raid or shallow breathing
Pain
Cyanosis or the blueing of skin colour 

## Slide 51

4.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR A CHEST INJURY

If their is a suspected rib fracture:
Help the casualty to sit down
Ask the casualty to support the arm 
on the injured side
Place the arm in a supported 
sling for extra support 
Caution: a casualty may guard 
the injury and may not allow 
you to administer treatment.

## Slide 52

4.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR A CHEST INJURY

If the wound is open ‘sucking chest wound’:
Call 999/112
Help the casualty to sit down, leaning towards the injured side
Apply a sterile plastic or non 
fluffy pad, taking care to tape it 
only on three sides (this will 
allow air to escape but prevent 
air from entering the chest 
cavity via the open wound 
causing a collapsed lung)

## Slide 53

4.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR A CHEST INJURY

If their is a penetrating object present: 
Stabilise with padding and dressing around the wound
Support the casualty and place 
the arm on the injured side in 
an elevated sling to elevate 
pressure 
Reassure the casualty 
and monitor for signs 
of shock or unconsciousness
