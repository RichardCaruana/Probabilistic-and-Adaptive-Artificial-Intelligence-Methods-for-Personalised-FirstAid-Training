## Slide 59

5.1 INTRODUCTION TO BURNS AND SCALDS 

Burns can sometimes be deep and cover a large area
Burns and scalds are caused as 
a result of contact with heat, 
radiation, electricity, chemicals 
or a freezing cold surface 
The longer the burn is left 
un-cooled, the more severe 
it will become. 
Your priority is to cool the burn
A person who has suffered severe burns and scalds will also be suffering from shock.

## Slide 65

5.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
FOR BURNS AND SCALDS 

The first aiders priorities are to: 
To cool the area and stop the burning 
To monitor the casualties airway
To minimise the risk of infection and the onset of shock
To get the casualty to hospital 
with urgency 
To obtain a good history 
of the incident for the 
emergency services

## Slide 66

5.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID
FOR BURNS AND SCALDS  

Cool
Cool the injury site with cold running water for at least 10 minutes
Do not use Ice
Do not apply any fats, creams or lotions
Do not burst any blisters 
Remove 
Remove any restricting items such as jewellery, watches, belts etc before the area starts to swell
Dress 
When the area is cooled sufficiently cover the burn to minimise risk of infection
Cling film is ideal or sterile non-fluffy dressings

## Slide 67

5.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR BURNS/SCALDS 

REMEMBER
Do NOT break blisters
Do NOT apply lotions
Do NOT remove 
burnt clothing
Do NOT apply 
adhesive dressings
Do NOT cover burns that may 
obstruct the airway

## Slide 68

5.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
FOR CHEMICAL BURNS

Wear personal protection 
Cool the area with cold running water for at least 20 minutes to dilute the chemical
Remove any contaminated clothing 
quickly but carefully
Apply sterile dressing 
and call the emergency 
services 

## Slide 69

 5.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
FOR BURNS TO THE AIRWAY

If fumes or smoke have been inhaled, there may be soot traces around the nose and mouth and possible difficulty in breathing 
Loosen tight clothing
Remove the casualty 
to fresh air
Place them in a seated 
position and monitor 
the airway and breathing
Dial 999/112 
for an ambulance 

## Slide 70

5.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
FOR BURNS CAUSED BY ELECTRICITY 

Electrical burns caused by domestic items, overhead power cables, lightening etc may cause entry and exit burns to the skin
Electric shock may cause cardiac arrest 
Once you have ensured the area is safe, treat the burns as per the normal procedure 
If the burn is severe 
(caused by high voltage)
- call 999/112. 
DO NOT approach and keep 
 distance  

## Slide 71

5.2 WHEN TO OBTAIN MEDICAL ASSISTANCE

If the burn is deep or large
If the burn is on the face, genital area, at joints, around the upper body, the neck or circumvents 
a limb
If the burn is caused by electricity or chemicals
If smoke or fumes 
have been inhaled
If the casualty is going 
into shock
If the victim is a child 
If the person is elderly
