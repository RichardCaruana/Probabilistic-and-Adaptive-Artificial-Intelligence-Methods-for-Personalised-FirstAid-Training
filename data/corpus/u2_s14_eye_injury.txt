## Slide 78

6.1 COMMON TYPES OF EYE INJURIES 

Eye injuries can result in permanent sight loss 
or severe damage to the casualties sight
Prevention is better than cure and therefore suitable provision should be taken in preventing an eye injury as far as possible 
Personal protective equipment should be worn where necessary 
Generally eye injuries fall into two categories:
minor 
or major 

## Slide 79

6.1 COMMON TYPES OF EYE INJURIES 

Minor eye injury - are most often caused by 
a foreign object irritating the surface of the eye
Major eye injury - are those that involve 
the penetration of the eye or severe blunt force trauma. 

## Slide 80

6.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR EYE INJURIES 

You need to: 
Limit further damage to the eye
Reassure and relieve anxiety 
Monitor the casualty 
Minimise the risk 
of the onset of shock 
Obtain a good history 
of the incident for 
the emergency services 

## Slide 81

6.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR EYE INJURIES 

Ask the casualty to sit down. Gently open the eye and check for any foreign bodies 
Flush the eye with clean water 
(or sterile eye wash) to wash 
out any foreign bodies. 
Take care to ensure 
the irrigation drains away 
from the good eye.
Seek medical assistance 
if appropriate. 

Minor injury 

## Slide 82

6.2 ADMINISTER FIRST AID FOR EYE INJURIES 

Call 999/112
Lay the casualty flat
Ask the casualty to cover 
the affected eye using 
a non fluffy dressing
Encourage the casualty 
to close the unaffected eye 
to minimise movement

Major injury
