## Slide 89

7.1 HOW POISONS CAN ENTER THE BODY

Poisons can enter the body by:
Inhalation
Ingestion
Injection
Absorption 

## Slide 90

7.2 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF POISONING 

Pale, cool and clammy skin
Vomiting 
Burns around the mouth
Irregular heartbeat
Blurred vision 
Confusion/hallucination 
Ringing in the ears 
Smell of fumes
Rash and/or swelling of the affected area 
Cyanosis- blue skin
Stomach pain or cramps 
Breathing problems 
Drowsiness 
Convulsions
Unconsciousness 

## Slide 91

7.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
TO A CASUALTY WITH SUDDEN POISONING

Absorbed poison (skin)
Call 999/112
Be prepared to resuscitate 
Remove the contaminated 
clothing  with care so as 
not to spread exposure
Flood the affected area 
with cool running water 
for at least 20 minutes

## Slide 92

7.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
TO A CASUALTY WITH SUDDEN POISONING

Absorbed poison (eye)
Call 999/112
Be prepared to resuscitate 
Gently holding the eye 
open, flood inside and 
out with running water 
for at least 10 minutes 
Cover the eye with 
a sterile dressing

## Slide 93

7.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
TO A CASUALTY WITH SUDDEN POISONING

Ingested Poison 
Call 999/112 
Be prepared to resuscitate 
Ask the casualty what 
they have ingested
If you have to give rescue 
breaths use a face shield 
for your own protection

## Slide 94

7.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
TO A CASUALTY WITH SUDDEN POISONING

Inhaled Poison 
Call 999/112 
Be prepared to resuscitate 
If possible move the casualty 
to fresh air and ventilate 
the area well

## Slide 95

7.2	ADMINISTER FIRST AID 
TO A CASUALTY WITH SUDDEN POISONING

Injected Poison 
Call 999/112 
Be prepared to resuscitate 
Remove the sting/needle 
if possible
Apply a cold compress

## Slide 96

7.3	SOURCES OF INFORMATION FOR TREATING 
THOSE AFFECTED BY SUDDEN POISONING 

Look for containers which might point towards what has been ingested/absorbed/inhaled/injected
and show them to the ambulance crew
Keep samples 
of any vomit for 
medical analysis
