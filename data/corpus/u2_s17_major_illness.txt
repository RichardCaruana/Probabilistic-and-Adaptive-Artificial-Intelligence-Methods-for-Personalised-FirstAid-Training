## Slide 112

9.1 HEART ATTACK

A sudden blood clot in a coronary artery which restricts the flow of blood to the heart is the most common cause of a heart attack
The amount of damage caused to the heart muscle by the restriction 
of oxygenated blood circulating 
around the heart, determinates
 the severity of the attack 
A heart attack can take place 
at any time. It could happen 
at work or even whilst asleep.

## Slide 113

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF A HEART ATTACK

A severe vice like pain in the centre of the chest that does not ease when the victim rests
The pain may radiate up into the jaw and/or down one or both arms
The victim may be breathless, gasping for air
The victim may have a pain high in the abdomen much like indigestion
The victim may become faint or dizzy 
and may collapse without warning
The victim may be cold and clammy 
to touch and sweating profusely
The victim may display signs 
of anxiety and have a sense 
of impending doom

## Slide 114

9.2 TREATMENT OF A HEART ATTACK

Help the casualty into a comfortable position. 
The ‘W’ position is ideal
Call 999/112 
If the casualty has their own medication, 
assist them to self-medicate
Reassure and monitor 
the casualty's breathing and pulse
Be prepared to administer CPR 
if necessary. 

## Slide 115

9.1 ANGINA 

This is a condition which develops over time and is caused by a build up of plaque narrowing the coronary artery which supplies blood to the heart. 
Angina is usually brought on by sudden exertion 
Sufferers of the condition are usually on medication
Rest is usually sufficient to ease the pain.

## Slide 116

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF ANGINA

Initial symptoms are similar to a heart attack
The pain will ease with rest
The casualty will have shortness of breath 
and a sudden tiredness which can be extreme 
The casualty may be anxious 
The casualty may insist that 
the condition is only angina, 
but be aware of any continuing 
pain and discomfort being 
experienced by the casualty, 
   which would indicate      
          a heart attack.

## Slide 117

9.2 TREATMENT OF ANGINA 

Make the casualty comfortable, reduce stress and stop any exertion
If the casualty has medication 
for his angina, help them 
to locate it and them take it. 
If necessary you may assist 
the casualty to take there 
medication 
The pain should ease with rest and medication. However if the pain continues longer than a few minutes or returns, suspect a heart attack. 

## Slide 118

9.1 STROKE 

A stroke is either a blocked or a ruptured blood vessel supplying oxygen to the brain
The pressure build up in either case causes 
the surrounding brain tissue to die resulting 
in permanent damage and even death
A stroke can occur without 
warning and can affect anyone 
regardless of age or lifestyle
The earlier the intervention, 
         the better the outcome

## Slide 119

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF A STROKE

Face
Arms
Speech
Time

## Slide 120

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF A STROKE

Face - has the casualty’s face fallen on one side? Can they smile? 
Arms - Can the casualty raise both arms and keep them raised? 
Speech - Is the casualty’s speech slurred? Can they understand and respond to what’s being said? 
Time - to call 999/112 if the casualty fails any one of these tests
Acting FAST can save the casualty’s life and encourage a full recovery.

## Slide 121

9.2 TREATMENT OF STROKE 

The first aiders aim is to:
Act FAST, arrange for the casualty to go to hospital immediately 
Prevent the casualty from 
doing themselves harm
To monitor the casualty 
To obtain a good history 
of the incident for 
the emergency services 

## Slide 122

9.2 TREATMENT OF STROKE 

If the casualty fails any one 
of the FAST tests, 
call 999/112 immediately
If conscious place the casualty in a comfortable position which does not obstruct the airway 
(the W position is preferred) 
If the casualty is unconscious, open the airway and check for breathing. If the casualty appears 
to be breathing, place in the recovery position. 
Reassure the casualty and talk to them, even 
if they are unconscious. Do not assume they 
do not understand. 

## Slide 123

9.1 MANAGE A CASUALTY WHO IS IN SEIZURE 

Seizures are caused by sudden, abnormal electrical activity in the brain. 
Seizures can be caused by 
reactions to medicines, 
high fevers, head injuries 
and certain diseases such as cancer 
of the brain or epilepsy can result in a seizure.
There are two types of seizure:
Tonic-clonic (referred 
to a ‘fit’ or ‘Grand-Mal’)
Absences (sometimes 
referred to as ‘Petit-Mal’)

## Slide 124

9.1 	SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS 
OF A SEIZURE (EXAMPLES)

Sudden loss of consciousness 
Staring blankly ahead 
Odd noises or movements/facial expressions 
Muscle rigidity followed 
by uncontrolled jerking
Breathing ceases but resumes 
once seizure has finished 
Clenched jaw and bloody 
saliva- casualty may bite tongue 
Incontinence 

## Slide 125

9.2 MANAGING A CASUALTY WHO IS IN SEIZURE 

Help the casualty to the floor to prevent injury 
(if they have not already fallen). 
Note the time of the seizure
Remove any potential dangers 
Remove any bystanders
Protect the head
Loosen any tight clothing
Wait, monitor and constantly reassure the casualty 
Call 999/112 if this is the casualty's first ever seizure, if the episode lasts for more than 5 minutes 
or if the casualty has been unconscious 
          for more than 10 minutes

## Slide 126

9.1 ASTHMA 

Asthma is an allergic reaction in the lungs caused by external stimuli such as pet hair, 
dust or pollen
Asthma can be fatal and there 
are more than 1,200 deaths 
a year, many of which may 
be entirely preventable 
Most asthmatics carry 
a reliever inhaler (usually blue) 
which has the effect of dilating 
the bronchioles thereby making 
breathing easier.

## Slide 128

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF ASTHMA 

Difficulty in breathing especially exhalation
Breathing that sounds ‘wheezy’
Tightness in the chest
Anxiety
Unable to speak fluently 
Cyanosis
Exhaustion
Unconsciousness 

## Slide 129

9.2 TREATMENT OF ASTHMA 

Sit the casualty down 
and lean them forward
Assist them 
in the use of the inhaler
Be calm and reassuring 
Encourage slow and deep breathing so as to minimise the risk of hyperventilation
If there is no improvement after two minutes, repeat steps 2-4
Call 999/112 if the attack is not relieved 
after 5 minutes
Be prepared to resuscitate

## Slide 130

9.1 DIABETES 

Hyperglycaemia (high blood sugar)

Develops when the body’s immune system attacks and destroys the cells that produce insulin
They will be insulin dependant and regularly inject themselves throughout their lives

Hypoglycaemia (low blood sugar)

Develops when the body does not produce enough insulin or is unable to effectively use the insulin that is being produced
Usually related to poor diet and obesity and most often controlled by wither changes to diet and/or tablets. 

Diabetes is caused by an imbalance of glucose 
in the blood as a result of insulin deficiency. 

## Slide 131

9.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS OF DIABETES

Paleness in skin colour
Profuse sweating
Extreme hunger/thirst
Confused, bizarre 
or violent behaviour 
Fruity sweet breath
Rapid pulse
Dizzy
Seizures 
Unconsciousness 

## Slide 132

9.2 TREATMENT FOR DIABETES 

If conscious, sit the casualty down and give them something to eat or drink which contains a high sugar content
Assist the casualty with the administration of their medication and help locate their glucose testing kit to check blood sugar levels 
If the condition does not improve 
call 999/112. Tell them you suspect hyperglycaemia
If unconscious place in the 
recovery position and monitor. 
DO NOT attempt to administer an insulin injection.
