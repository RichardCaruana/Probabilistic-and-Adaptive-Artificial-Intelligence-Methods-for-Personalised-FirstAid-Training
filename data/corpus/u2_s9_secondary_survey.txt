## Slide 5

1.1	IDENTIFY THE INFORMATION TO BE COLLECTED 
WHEN GATHERING A CASUALTY HISTORY 

Once the primary survey has been completed 
and you are sure that the casualty is not in a life threatening condition, you must now conduct 
a secondary survey 
The secondary survey should 
be completed as swiftly as 
possible and prior to placing 
the casualty in the recovery position
The secondary survey 
consists of two parts
The casualties history, signs and symptoms 
Top to toe survey

## Slide 7

1.1 TAKING A SAMPLE HISTORY (SAMPLE) 

Signs and symptoms

What can you see? How does the casualty feel?

Allergies 

Does the casualty have any allergies? 

Medication

Is the casualty taking any medication? 

Past history

Does the casualty have any previous medical conditions or illness? 

Last intake

When did the casualty last eat? 

Event history

How did the casualty  feel leading up until the accident/illness? 

## Slide 8

1.1 SIGNS AND SYMPTOMS 

Sign
A sign is something you can see, smell and hear e.g. A lump or cerebral spinal fluid (CSF).

Symptom
A  symptom is something that a casualty describes how they are feeling e.g. My back hurts, dizziness, nausea etc.
